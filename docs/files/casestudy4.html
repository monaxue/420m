Dataset · ↗ · ⤓

You work as a statistical consultant for a medical care corporation, and part of your job is to assess patients’ cognitive abilities. Your company is testing out a perceptual task that involves mental rotation, which is the ability to imagine what an object would look like from a different perspective. During the task, the patient is first shown an image of an object and then is asked to determine if a second presented object is the same as the first – merely rotated – or an entirely new object. They complete 100 such trials, 50 where the objects are the same and 50 where they are different. (See the figure below for an example.)

Your company would like to use this task to help diagnose individuals that have undergone cognitive decline. Previous research has established that in healthy individuals, the speed (in milliseconds) at which someone can identify that the second object is the same as the first depends on how much the object has been rotated (measured in degrees); moreover, the relationship is approximately linear. For impaired individuals, the relationship between speed and degree of rotation is approximately flat (i.e., they are unassociated).

Five patients have completed the task described above. Since you are only interested in the cases where the two objects were indeed the same, you have only been given the 50 trials from that condition. Analyzing each patient individually, plot the completion time as a function of the degree of rotation and produce an estimate of the slope (including a 95% confidence interval). Your company would like to know which patients, if any, show evidence of cognitive decline. Produce a short report where you summarize the data, present descriptive results, and draw conclusions about each patient’s cognitive status.
